### L’essentiel de l’épisode
Ce tour d’horizon de la scène indépendante et underground chinoise récente met en lumière une vitalité créative largement décentralisée, hors des circuits commerciaux traditionnels de Pékin ou Shanghai. La sélection privilégie des projets issus de scènes provinciales (Sichuan, Xinjiang, Guizhou, Gansu) caractérisés par des hybridations stylistiques radicales : post-punk montagnard, black metal teinté de free jazz, réinterprétations folkloriques kazakhes et pop confessionnelle en dialecte régional. L'ensemble esquisse le portrait d'une génération d'artistes tournée vers le DIY, l'expérimentation sonore brute et l'expression d'un désenchantement intime.

## Post-Punk Collaborations and Xinjiang Sounds

Hello, my name is Jake and I write a free newsletter about music from China called **Concrete Avalanche**. Over the next hour or so, **I'm going to be playing some of my favourite music to come out of China in the first half of this year**, beginning with this piece from Puku Plaza. This is the title track from the EP *Undercurrent*.

That was Lagort Quartet with a song from the album *Through Voodoo Light to the Shore of Tomorrow*. Lagort Quartet features **Yang Haisong and Shi Xudong of Chinese post-punk pioneers PK-14**, alongside Sleeping Dogs drummer Li Zichao and Ji Yinan of Chengdu post-punks Hiperson. The album was mostly recorded over the course of a few days that the band had been holed up in a small house up a mountain in Sichuan.

Before that, we had the brilliant Xinjiang band **Three Mice**, whose debut full-length album, *Where Is My Mice*, is absolutely full of gems like that one, switching between angsty emo and sweetly strummed indie-rock melodies. It's a fantastic record and **it's also pay-what-you-want on Bandcamp**, so you should definitely go and pick that up if you haven't already.

Something a little different up next: we're going to hear a song from **Xinjiang-born Kazakh multi-instrumentalist Mamer**, whose latest album, *All the Joys, All the Pains*, features interpretations of classic Kazakh lullabies, traditional poems, laments for lost loved ones, and 1980s radio hits.

## Avant-Garde Improvisation and Ambient Landscapes

Before that, this is an excerpt from a piece by Gogo J, aka Sheng Jie, and Deng Boyu, two stalwarts of the Chinese avant-garde music scene. The pair, with Sheng Jie on electric cello and Deng providing percussion, **recorded two improvised takes based on the same structure and framework, but then couldn't decide which one they liked best, so simply released both**. The result is this album, which is called *HEH*—or H-E-H, I'm not really sure how they're pronouncing it, but anyway, this is a bit of track B1.

That was a Wuhan-based singer-songwriter who really blew up with a DIY album, *No-No*, which merged rock, pop and more, and **topped a lot of the best-of-year lists in 2024**. The track there was from her follow-up full-length record, *Catch My Boomerang*, and that track was called "The World Is Dry."

Up next, we're going to start in ambient territory with a track from Wu Zhuoling, **a figure who has played a major role in shaping Chengdu and China's musical landscape over the past two decades**. Partly inspired by a UFO encounter she had as a child in Sichuan, she released the EP *Uncharted 2* at the end of March. The track you can hear coming in in the background right now is taken from the record, and it's called "Gaia's Breath."

## From Emo Revival to Solo Black Metal

After this, we'll hear a song from Cassette Player Romance before going somewhere else entirely. Giorgio and Guo Yinhan caused a lot of buzz back in March with their album **_The Vortex Reconstruction Experiment_**, which blends youthful disenchantment and a swirl of samples featuring everyone from Marina Herlop to Sam Wilkes.

Before that, we heard the one that sounds like Sweet Trip from Cassette Player Romance, who sounds maybe a little different to the track that came after it, but who actually has strong links to the alternative Chinese hip-hop scene as well, producing beats under his alias Soul Looper. **He's also a key figure in China's emo revival**, playing in the bands Jiujian 250 and the Beneficial Society.

Next up is another impressive solo act. **Dead Trees is a one-man black metal act from Lanzhou** who is well worth paying attention to. His new album, which works in elements of free jazz alongside some punchy percussion, is called *New World*, and this song is "Extinguished City."

## Hyper-Pop Confessions from Guizhou

Closing out our mid-year roundup for ChinaTalk with a track from Guizhou, from a hyper-pop producer known as DJ Girl. The artist behind that persona put out a new album earlier this year, but it's something quite different. As Lear, he's picked up the mic and **delivered an intimately confessional album, discussing his upbringing, heartbreak and his sexuality, all in a heavy Guizhou accent** that makes me think of the film *Kaili Blues*.

Just before I go, thank you to ChinaTalk for having me back, and thank you for listening. You can find lots more interesting music from China on Concrete Avalanche, and if you've enjoyed any of the tracks that you've heard here, please follow the links to them and support the artists by buying their music if you're able to.
